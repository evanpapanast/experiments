What we found

How to use this

  • If the ECB sounds worried about the economy for 2 or 3 months, expect a possible rate cut. Tell your finance or investment team early.
  • Do not act on tough talk about inflation alone. It usually explains a rise that has already happened. Check the price and wage figures first.
  • Listen most carefully after a long pause in rate changes. This is when guessing "the same as last time" fails, and when the speeches helped most.
  • Use speeches as one clue, not the only one. There were only 9 changes of direction in 27 years, so word counts alone cannot predict rates.

This is an analysis of public data. It is not investment advice.

How we tested it

  • Rules first, results after. We wrote the word lists and the rules before looking at any interest rates. So we could not pick words that fit the answer.
  • Tough tone = phrases about prices rising too fast (for example "upside risks", "inflationary pressures"). Soft tone = phrases about a weak economy (for example "downside risks", "subdued").
  • Tone score = tough phrases minus soft phrases, over the last 3 months of speeches. We compare it with the usual score of the 2 years before, because the ECB's words change slowly over time.
  • Interest rate = the main ECB rate until May 2014, then the deposit rate (the rate that matters since rates went below zero). Source: ECB statistics. The two official rates are used exactly as published; joining them into one line, and leaving out small technical changes, is our own step.
  • "Clearly tough" or "clearly soft" = the 15% of months with the toughest or softest tone. A hint in advance = the tone pointed the right way in at least 2 of the 3 months before the decision.

      How it was built

      ECB speeches CSV→Python: clean and label→ Totals only→Interactive report

      Share of attention = how many times the words of one subject appear, divided by the words of all subjects. It shows how the ECB splits its attention, not how much it talks. The main subject of a speech is the one with the most matching words. "Climate change" counts once, not as "climate" plus "climate change".

      Privacy and copyright

      • No speech text. The ECB does not allow copying signed speeches without permission. So we only publish counts and percentages.
      • No names. Speaker names and speech titles are never saved. Each speech only has a number. Names are also removed from the word search.
      • Source named. The ECB is named as the source. All figures are my own calculations.

      Limits

      • Word rules do not understand meaning: "the crisis is over" still counts as crisis talk.
      • Shares are relative: a subject can lose share even if it is mentioned as often as before.
      • Two subjects are still missing: financial integration and competition. We found this when checking by hand.
