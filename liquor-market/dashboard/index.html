    The business question

    For a company selling spirits in Iowa: are sales growing because people buy more, or only because prices rise? How good was the budget? What should we plan for next year, and where should we act?

    The data

    Every bottle of spirits that Iowa stores buy goes through the state, which publishes each order. Source: Iowa Department of Revenue, Alcohol Operations Bureau, Iowa Liquor Sales, licensed under CC BY 4.0; read from the Google BigQuery public datasets. Store, supplier and product names are in the source but are not kept: the report uses totals by product family and month only.

    How it was built

    • BigQuery: the 44 detailed categories are grouped into 11 product families; a monthly table is built straight from the public data and checked against it (sales, litres and order lines match exactly).
    • Price, volume and mix: price = sales per litre. Volume effect = change in litres at last year's average price; mix = effect of selling more of the dearer or cheaper families; price = change in price per litre within each family.
    • Forecast: BigQuery's built-in TimesFM model (AI.FORECAST), one forecast per family for sales and for litres. The "budget" is the forecast made with data up to December of the last full year, so it can be tested against what really happened.

    • Cost: all work fits in BigQuery's free tier (the largest query read about 2 GB; the free tier is 1 TB a month).

    Limits

    • These are sales from the state to stores, not from stores to shoppers. They show what stores order, which follows what shoppers buy with a short delay.
    • The forecast model learns from past patterns. It was too optimistic this year; treat it as the top of the range.
